# Baseline connectivity inference across the sweep datasets

Branch `baselines-comparison`. Every number below was produced by `analysis/baselines/run_dataset.py` (per dataset) and aggregated by `analysis/baselines/aggregate.py` into `metrics.csv` (long format: session, state, method, arm, readout, operating_point, denominator, metric, value). Figures are listed with the script and operating point that produced them. Datasets: 20 networks x normal/seizure state(s), 200 recordings each (n200).

## Design

- **Same sufficient statistics, three normalisations.** `analysis/baselines/accumulate.py` builds, in one pass over per-recording sparse products, the lag products `C[l][pre, post]` (the raw cross-correlogram counts). The ridge GLM's right-hand side `F X^T` at lag l *is* `C[l]`; the raw counts feed the correlogram methods, the rate-normalised counts feed Pearson, and the precision-whitened solve `(F F^T + lambda I)^-1 F X^T` is the shipped GLM. `test_accumulate.py` shows the solve is bit-identical to `sparse_glm.fit_B` and to the shipped `glm_connectivity_sum4_5ms.npz`, so the GLM row is reproduced, not re-fit.
- **Two passes.** 5 ms bins with lags 1-6 (the shipped GLM, lagged/zero-lag Pearson, jittered CCG) and 1 ms bins with lags 0-50 (classical CCG, TSPE, FNCCH, GLMCC, STG, TE at 1 ms; TE at 2/5/10 ms uses its own pass).
- **Invariants.** Every matrix is `[pre, post]`; the candidate set is every ordered off-diagonal pair for every method; ground truth is read only in `evaluate.py`; positive = excitatory; every F1 and recall is reported under two denominators, `all_edges` (A_exc | A_inh) and `exc_only` (A_exc, an inhibitory hit counted as a false positive).
- **Read-outs.** `exc` (+S vs excitatory edges), `inh` (-S vs inhibitory edges), `all_absS` (|S| vs all edges, the untyped protocol), `typed` (rank rule, assumed inhibitory fraction 0.25: +S on rows typed excitatory, -S on rows typed inhibitory, vs all edges).
- **Operating points.** Threshold-free first (AUC, AP). `oracle_best_f1` picks each method's own best-F1 threshold *using labels* -- an upper bound, labelled as such. `label_free_*` is the method's own published rule from spikes alone, where it defines one (jitter-null FDR 0.70 for the GLM and the jittered CCG; Poisson flank test for the classical CCG; mu+k sigma for FNCCH; |J| > J_thr for GLMCC; Poisson ROI test for STG). Pearson, TSPE and TE define none.
- **Arms.** `full` = all recordings; `burst_excluded` = burst windows recomputed at the 0.35 participation gate, padded +/-25 ms, removed from every pass with each retained interval its own segment. Stored burst windows are never loaded.
- **Delay-recovery gate.** Before the sweep, every directed method with a lag-resolved evidence profile was checked on one dataset (n = 50): the lag of peak evidence on true excitatory edges must correlate with the true conduction delay (1.5 + 2.0 x distance ms) at r >= 0.3, and the transposed profile must not do better.

## Key findings (computed from the tables below)

**normal state, full recordings (mean over networks).**
- Excitatory AP: ridge GLM 0.941 vs best published baseline TSPE 0.795 (then classical CCG 0.724, FNCCH 0.721, TE 1 ms 0.479); untyped AP 0.873 vs 0.727.
- Mismatch probe STG ROI 15 ms: exc AP 0.812, untyped AP 0.740 (vs the faithful STG (deconv.) 0.252 / 0.285).
- Mismatch probe GLMCC d<=14 ms: exc AP 0.792, untyped AP 0.219 (vs the faithful GLMCC 0.361 / 0.149).
- Label-free operating points (each method's own rule, untyped, all-edges denominator): ridge GLM P 0.794 / R 0.808; jittered CCG P 0.041 / R 0.963; GLMCC P 0.309 / R 0.079; STG (deconv.) P 0.423 / R 0.282; FNCCH P 0.202 / R 0.606; classical CCG P 0.121 / R 0.921; STG ROI 15 ms P 0.573 / R 0.745; GLMCC d<=14 ms P 0.155 / R 0.166.
- Same statistic, same null: with the identical +/-25 ms x 8-surrogate jitter null at nominal FDR 0.70, the raw-CCG row realizes FDR 0.959 (AP 0.347) while the whitened GLM row realizes 0.206 (AP 0.873). The joint fit is what makes the null usable, not the null itself.
- Burst exclusion (untyped AP, burst-excluded minus full): 1 ms-window methods -0.026 on average (classical CCG +0.01, TSPE -0.05, FNCCH -0.06, TE 1 ms +0.05, STG ROI 15 ms -0.08), 5-10 ms-window methods +0.141 (jittered CCG +0.22, lagged Pearson +0.08, TE 5 ms +0.12, TE 10 ms +0.13); ridge GLM -0.028 in AP but label-free precision 0.794 -> 0.906. Removing burst windows removes the shared drive that swamps coarse-timescale statistics, and removes the coincidence counts that the fine-timescale statistics need.

## Compute: projected vs spent, subsampling, failures

The sweep was executed LOCALLY (24-core workstation, 62 GB) with analysis/baselines/run_sweep.py rather than on CHTC: the pilot showed every method finishes a dataset in minutes, not hours, so the CHTC kit (chtc/make_baselines_manifest.py, baselines.sub, baselines_job.sh, baselines_one.py, collect_baselines.py; one job per (dataset, method)) is provided and tested for manifest generation but was not submitted. ## LIMITATIONS: what each method was validated on, and how this network differs

This network: 629-1010 single-compartment HH neurons in 40-50 spatial clusters, log-normal weights, 1.5-2.1 % directed edge density, conduction delays 1.5-13.5 ms (1.5 + 2.0 x distance), mean rates 0.29 Hz (normal) and 1.7 Hz (seizure), network bursts 9-25 per minute with 51-95 % participation; 200 x 60 s per dataset.

- **ridge GLM (shipped)** (this repository (sparse lag-resolved ridge GLM, sum4 readout, jitter-null FDR 0.70)). Developed on this simulator; every hyperparameter (5 ms bins, lags 1-6, l2 2.0, sum4, jitter 25 ms, FDR 0.70) was chosen on earlier sessions of the same model family. It is the in-domain method; that is the reviewer objection the oracle columns are there to answer.
- **jittered CCG** (Fujisawa et al. 2008, Nat Neurosci 11:823; Amarasingham et al. 2012, J Neurophysiol 107:517). Fujisawa et al. 2008 and Amarasingham et al. 2012 used 1 ms bins, +/-5 ms jitter and ~1000 surrogates on in vivo prefrontal / motor recordings with tens of units. Here the null is the shipped GLM's (5 ms bins, +/-25 ms, 8 surrogates) by design, so this row isolates the joint fit rather than reproducing the published operating point.
- **GLMCC** (Kobayashi et al. 2019, Nat Commun 10:4468 (github.com/NII-Kobayashi/GLMCC)). Kobayashi et al. 2019 validated GLMCC on 1000 Hodgkin-Huxley neurons recorded for 5400 s and on rat CA1 in vivo (hc-3), with synaptic delays of a few ms and tau = 4 ms. Its default delay search (1-4 ms) covers only the shortest conduction delays of this network (1.5-13.5 ms); the glmcc_d14 probe widens it. The per-pair background a(t) absorbs slow common input but nothing shared across pairs, and the J_thr rule assumes independent Poisson-like backgrounds.
- **STG (deconv.)** (Spivak et al. 2022, Commun Biol 5:520 (ROI 0-5 ms as published)). Spivak et al. 2022 validated the deconvolved STG on point-process simulations, a 100-neuron CA1 model and 1041 PYR / 215 INT recorded in mouse CA1, with a 0-5 ms region of interest suited to hippocampal monosynaptic latencies. Most conduction delays here are longer than 5 ms, so the faithful ROI sees a minority of true edges; stg_roi15 is the widened probe.
- **TSPE** (De Blasi et al. 2019, J Neurosci Methods 312:169 (github.com/biomemsLAB/TSPE)). De Blasi et al. 2019 validated TSPE on simulated cortical cultures (Izhikevich networks of ~1000 neurons, low connection density, 1 ms bins) and on MEA culture recordings. Delays up to 25 ms are covered, so the 1.5-13.5 ms conduction delays here are inside its window; the clustered, burst-dominated dynamics of this model are not what it was tuned on, and it defines no label-free threshold (its authors chose thresholds by ROC).
- **FNCCH** (Pastore et al. 2018, PLoS Comput Biol 14:e1006381). Pastore et al. 2018 validated FNCCH on 1000-neuron in silico random networks (1 h) and on cortical cultures on MEA-60 and MEA-4k arrays (0.12 ms bins on the 4k array). The +/-25 ms window covers the delays here; the mu+2sigma / mu+1sigma hard thresholds were tuned for their density and recording length.
- **classical CCG** (Perkel, Gerstein & Moore 1967, Biophys J 7:419). Perkel et al. 1967 validated the correlogram on pairs of neurons recorded in isolation, not on densely interconnected populations; the flank baseline cannot remove common input and the authors themselves discuss the resulting false inferences. Poisson test constants (alpha 0.001, Bonferroni over 15 causal bins) are ours.
- **TE 1 ms** (Schreiber 2000, PRL 85:461; Ito et al. 2011, PLoS ONE 6:e27431 (1 ms bins)). Ito et al. 2011 validated delayed TE on a 1000-neuron Izhikevich cortical model (100 subsampled neurons, 30 min, 7.3 % density) and thresholded by ROC. The bin width is known to matter (their Fig. on bin size), hence the 1/2/5/10 ms sweep; k = l = 1 histories are used (the higher-order extension is not run). The sign attached here is an addition: TE is unsigned.
- **TE 2 ms** (as te_1ms, 2 ms bins). Ito et al. 2011 validated delayed TE on a 1000-neuron Izhikevich cortical model (100 subsampled neurons, 30 min, 7.3 % density) and thresholded by ROC. The bin width is known to matter (their Fig. on bin size), hence the 1/2/5/10 ms sweep; k = l = 1 histories are used (the higher-order extension is not run). The sign attached here is an addition: TE is unsigned.
- **TE 5 ms** (as te_1ms, 5 ms bins). Ito et al. 2011 validated delayed TE on a 1000-neuron Izhikevich cortical model (100 subsampled neurons, 30 min, 7.3 % density) and thresholded by ROC. The bin width is known to matter (their Fig. on bin size), hence the 1/2/5/10 ms sweep; k = l = 1 histories are used (the higher-order extension is not run). The sign attached here is an addition: TE is unsigned.
- **TE 10 ms** (as te_1ms, 10 ms bins). Ito et al. 2011 validated delayed TE on a 1000-neuron Izhikevich cortical model (100 subsampled neurons, 30 min, 7.3 % density) and thresholded by ROC. The bin width is known to matter (their Fig. on bin size), hence the 1/2/5/10 ms sweep; k = l = 1 histories are used (the higher-order extension is not run). The sign attached here is an addition: TE is unsigned.
- **lagged Pearson** (lagged Pearson correlation, max |r| over lags 1-6 at 5 ms, signed). No source paper. Rate normalisation only; nothing explains away shared drive, so its burst sensitivity is the reference for the correlation family.
- **zero-lag Pearson** (zero-lag Pearson correlation of 5 ms binned trains (undirected)). No source paper; undirected by construction. Included as the floor every other method must clear. It is scored on the directed candidate set, so its ceiling is well below 1.
- **STG ROI 15 ms** (Spivak et al. 2022 with the ROI widened to 0-15 ms (mismatch probe)). Spivak et al. 2022 validated the deconvolved STG on point-process simulations, a 100-neuron CA1 model and 1041 PYR / 215 INT recorded in mouse CA1, with a 0-5 ms region of interest suited to hippocampal monosynaptic latencies. Most conduction delays here are longer than 5 ms, so the faithful ROI sees a minority of true edges; stg_roi15 is the widened probe.
- **GLMCC d<=14 ms** (Kobayashi et al. 2019 with delays 1-14 ms (mismatch probe)). Kobayashi et al. 2019 validated GLMCC on 1000 Hodgkin-Huxley neurons recorded for 5400 s and on rat CA1 in vivo (hc-3), with synaptic delays of a few ms and tau = 4 ms. Its default delay search (1-4 ms) covers only the shortest conduction delays of this network (1.5-13.5 ms); the glmcc_d14 probe widens it. The per-pair background a(t) absorbs slow common input but nothing shared across pairs, and the J_thr rule assumes independent Poisson-like backgrounds.

### Skipped methods

- **connect**: CoNNECT needs a convolutional net pre-trained on statistics matched to the target network; no matched model exists for this simulator.
- **ising**: Ising / kinetic-Ising couplings estimate synchrony, are bin-width unstable, and are not directed in the sense the ground truth requires.
- **granger**: The review's own STG validation found Granger causality inconsistent with known physiology where the GLM was consistent.

## On the conservatism of the jitter null

The label-free operating point of the shipped GLM realizes an FDR far below its nominal target (section 2.3 of the thesis: 20-250x below nominal). Kobayashi et al. (2019), the GLMCC paper, report the same property of the jitter method on their ground-truth simulations: it "avoided making FPs, but missed many existing connections, in particular for inhibitory connections", and the 2025 review (Kobayashi & Shinomoto, Neurosci Res 215:37) restates it as the method being "too conservative". The conservatism is therefore a known property of jitter-based thresholding, not an idiosyncrasy of this pipeline. The `ccg_jitter` row applies the identical null to the raw correlogram; the gap between that row and the `glm_ridge` row is what the joint whitened fit adds on top of the null.
